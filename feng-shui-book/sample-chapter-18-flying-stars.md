# Chapter 18 — Xuan Kong Flying Stars: Feng Shui in Motion

*Part V · The Flying Stars*

> *When a thing reaches its limit, it changes; through change, it endures.*
>
> — from the Great Commentary on the *I Ching*

### The house that changed its mind

There is a house on a street you may know. For twenty years it was the fortunate house — the family within it prospered, the children did well, the garden seemed to lean toward the sun. Then, with no crack in the walls and no change written into the deeds, the luck went out of it. The next family struggled there. Nothing you could photograph had altered. And yet everyone on the street agreed: the house was no longer what it had been.

Every map you have built so far in this book is a map of *space*. The Bagua laid over your floor plan, the Eight Mansions and your Kua number — these describe where things are, and where things are does not change. They are the fixed skeleton of a home, and a skeleton holds still.

But a home does not only sit in space. It also travels through time. And as it travels, its energy turns — slowly, on a great wheel, the way a season turns. The fortunate house did not lose its luck. Its luck *moved* — because luck, in this tradition, is always moving. This chapter opens the dimension the earlier maps could not show.

This is the study of **Xuan Kong Flying Star** Feng Shui. *Xuan Kong* (玄空) means, near enough, *the mysterious void* — and it is the school that reads time and space as a single woven cloth. It is the most intricate system in this book, and also the most alive. The chapters that follow this one will teach you to cast your own home's star chart (Chapter 20), to turn its annual wheel (Chapter 21), and to cure it (Chapter 22). This chapter asks something prior, and more important than any calculation: that you learn to *think* in Flying Stars — to feel your home as a thing in motion.

### Energy keeps time

We met, in Chapter 4, the central teaching of the *I Ching*, the Book of Changes: that change is the one thing which does not change. The Flying Stars are that teaching forged into an instrument.

The spatial systems hold still. A home's facing direction, a person's Kua number — these are fixed for as long as the walls stand. They are the still skeleton. The Flying Stars are laid over that skeleton like weather moving across a settled landscape. Recall the Lo Shu (Chapter 11) — the sacred square in which the numbers one through nine are arranged so that every row, every column, and every diagonal sums to fifteen. The Flying Stars are those same nine numbers — but set loose to move. They travel across the nine palaces of a home (Chapter 12) along a fixed and ancient path, and they shift upon the cycles of time: the great cycle of twenty-year Periods, which Chapter 19 will set out, and the smaller, faster wheel of the single year, which Chapter 21 will turn.

The consequence is simple to say and slow to absorb: the number presiding over your wealth this year will preside over something else the next. A home, read in Flying Stars, is never finished being read.

### The nine stars

The nine numbers are not nine quantities. They are nine *characters* — each with an element, a temperament, and a way of behaving when it enters a room. They carry ancient names drawn from the nine stars of the Northern Dipper, and a practitioner comes, in time, to know them as one knows the people of a long-familiar street. Here they are.

**1 White** — *element Water.* The star of the scholar and of victory. Where it is welcome, it brings clear thinking, advancement in one's work, and recognition honestly earned. A quietly auspicious star.

**2 Black** — *element Earth.* The star of illness. When it falls out of its time it settles into the body as sickness and into the mind as worry. It is among the stars a practitioner learns to locate first, and to quiet.

**3 Jade** — *element Wood.* The quarrelsome star. It carries argument, disagreement, and the kind of misunderstanding that hardens into a lawsuit. Where the 3 is disturbed, households bicker and neighbors fall out.

**4 Green** — *element Wood.* The star of romance and of the scholar's brush. An auspicious star — it favors love and courtship, study, writing, and every kind of creative work.

**5 Yellow** — *element Earth.* The most feared of the nine: the Five Yellow, *Wu Huang*. It carries misfortune, obstruction, accident, and grave illness — and, alone among the nine, it has no good season, no era in which it turns kind. Wherever the Five Yellow travels, the whole of the practitioner's task is to subdue it. (You will learn how in Chapter 22. For now, only learn to respect it.)

**6 White** — *element Metal.* The star of heaven and of authority. It brings mentors and powerful friends, leadership, the regard of others, and the wealth that arrives through standing and influence. Auspicious.

**7 Red** — *element Metal.* The star of violence and robbery — theft, betrayal, deception, the sharp word and the sharp blade. Hold this star in your mind: we return to it in a moment, for the 7 has a lesson to teach that none of the others can.

**8 White** — *element Earth.* The great wealth star — prosperity, accumulation, gain that is solid and that endures. The 8 was the ruling star of the age just ended, the Period of 8, which ran from 2004 to 2023.

**9 Purple** — *element Fire.* The star of future prosperity, and the star that multiplies: it magnifies whatever it sits beside, brightening a blessing and — be warned — also deepening a trouble. It carries fame, celebration, and ripening wealth. The 9 is the ruling star of the present age, the Period of 9, 2024 to 2043.

### The turning of timeliness

Now the idea upon which the whole system rests — and the one a newcomer most often gets wrong.

Reading the list above, it is tempting to sort the nine stars into two drawers: the good — 1, 4, 6, 8, 9 — and the bad — 2, 3, 5, 7. Do not. With the single exception of the Five Yellow, **no star is simply good or simply bad. A star's nature is governed by time.**

Each star has its era — its Period — in which it is *timely*: the tradition calls this state *wang*, meaning prosperous, ruling, ascendant. In its own era a star's energy is current and rising, at full and benevolent strength. A timely auspicious star pours out fortune; and even a timely difficult star is gentled, its harm turned mild. Outside its era a star is *untimely* — retreating, its energy spent and beginning to sour. An untimely auspicious star merely dims and grows faint. But an untimely troublesome star comes fully into its harm.

Consider the 7 Red. Through the Period of 7 — the twenty years from 1984 to 2003 — the 7 was the ruling star, timely, and in those two decades it did not rob and did not betray. It brought wealth and success; a practitioner of that age welcomed the 7 gladly into a home. Then Period 7 ended, and the 7, now untimely, sank back into its underlying nature. Today it is once more a star of robbery and treachery. The 7 itself did not change. *The hour changed.*

By that same turning, the 8 — the great wealth star — reigned from 2004 to 2023 and now stands just past its height: still kindly, but no longer the king. And the 9 is the star of our own age. To know, then, whether a particular star in your home blesses you or troubles you, you must know two things and not one: *which* star it is, and *what hour it is* by the great clock. Chapter 19 will read you that clock; Chapter 20 will place the stars within your own walls; Chapter 21 will turn the annual wheel; and Chapter 22 will set the cures in your hand.

Here the Flying Stars give back, in exact and working form, the teaching the epigraph set down. Nothing beneath a roof is fortunate forever, and nothing is cursed for good. The practitioner does not cling to a fortunate star, nor grieve over an unfortunate one. She learns the hour, and she moves with it. This is not a gloomy doctrine. It is the most hopeful one in this book: a difficult chart is never a sentence — only a season, and seasons turn.

### A teahouse and its borrowed luck

> On a busy lane there once stood a teahouse that everyone called lucky. For twenty years old Madam Wen had kept it, and in her hands it had thrived — the tables always full — and she retired wealthy and content. When a younger man took over the rooms, he took them gladly, certain he had bought a fortune along with the furniture. He changed almost nothing. He kept her counter where it had always stood, kept the good table in the favored corner, kept the door's welcome facing the way it had always faced.
>
> Within three years he was failing, and he had begun to say that the lane had turned against him, or that the rooms themselves had soured.
>
> The practitioner he called did not think the rooms had soured. She asked him one question first — *when did Madam Wen's good years run?* — and then she explained. "Your predecessor did not own the luck of this house. She rented it, from a particular twenty years. When her lease ran out, so did theirs. The wealth that filled her tables gathered in that corner because a prosperous star was timely there — in *her* age. That age has ended. The ruling star of this new age has flown to another part of these rooms entirely, and you have kept your counter, your finest table, and your doorway all bowing to a corner the fortune has already left."
>
> She did not tell him to sell, and she did not tell him to despair. She told him where, in the present age, the prosperity now sat — and to turn the daily life of the teahouse to face it. The rooms had not betrayed him. He had only been keeping faith with a clock that had stopped.

### The seeing comes first

You are not yet able to read your home's stars. That is the work of the four chapters just ahead, and they will lead you through it one careful step at a time — the periods, the casting of the chart, the annual visitors, the cures. If the system looks intricate from here, take heart: it is learned the way a language is learned, a little at a time, until one day it simply reads.

What this chapter has given you is the thing all those steps would be useless without — the right way of seeing. A home is not a fixed fortune handed to you with the keys. It is a fortune in motion: nine traveling characters, crossing nine rooms, upon the slow wheel of the years. Hold that picture steady. Everything technical that follows is only the means of reading it.

> ### Practice — Reading the Clock of Your Home
>
> You cannot yet cast your home's star chart — but you can begin, today, to think in the way that casting it will require.
>
> 1. **Find two years.** Learn the year your home was built, and the year of its last major renovation — a renovation substantial enough to open the roof or move walls. Write both down. These two dates are the seed of the natal chart you will cast in Chapter 20.
> 2. **Place your home in its age.** Using the table of Periods in Chapter 19, note which twenty-year Period your home was "born" into. That Period set the home's enduring character.
> 3. **Recall the runs of fortune.** As far as you are able — from your own memory, from former occupants, from neighbors — trace how the household has fared across the years: the good stretches and the hard ones. Set them on a simple timeline.
> 4. **Mark the doorway of 2024.** That year opened the Period of 9. Ask whether anything in the household's fortunes — health, work, mood, money — shifted in the seasons around it.
> 5. **Write the home's hour.** In your practice journal, set down one honest paragraph that begins: *"My home keeps time as follows..."*
>
> You will not, at the end, hold a chart. You will hold something the chart needs first: the habit of seeing your home as a traveler through time — and not as a fixed and finished thing.
